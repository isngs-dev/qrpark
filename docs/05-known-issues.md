| T13 | Closed (T13 step) | Hardening H1 (2026-11-09) | The portal signs in from its own server, so the backend counts every portal sign-in under the PORTAL's address. Anyone on the public portal can block an account *through the portal* for 15 minutes with 5 wrong passwords (the username + address count becomes username + portal), and 30 failures through the portal from anyone block every portal sign-in. Direct callers (the app) are unaffected: their own addresses are counted (shown in the H1 evidence). Related: devices behind one market Wi-Fi share one public address, so 30 failed sign-ins across all of them in 15 minutes block sign-in on every device there. | DECISION PENDING. Recommended: the portal sends the browser's address in a header signed with a secret shared only by the portal and the backend; the backend uses that address only when the signature verifies, else its own `req.ip`. Alternatives: exempt the portal's address from the counts (weaker), or accept it. **Closed (2026-11-09)**: the recommended fix. The portal signs the browser's address (rightmost X-Forwarded-For entry) with `QRPARK_CLIENT_ADDRESS_SECRET`, shared with the backend; the backend believes it only on `POST /auth/login`, with a valid signature (constant-time) within 60 seconds, else uses `req.ip`. Proven: 5 wrong portal sign-ins from one browser do not block the same account from another browser. The market Wi-Fi point (shared public address) is unchanged. |
